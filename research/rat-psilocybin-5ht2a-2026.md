## Rat Study: Delayed 5-HT2A Protein Increase

**Study:** Wawrzczak-Bargieła et al., *Long-term administration of low doses of psilocybin causes epigenetic changes in the rat medial prefrontal cortex*, Psychopharmacology, published 28 July 2026.  
**Primary source:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-026-07132-6  
**Scientific wording reviewed:** 11 October 2026  
**Evidence class:** Controlled animal study; distinct from the human survey and community observations above.

### Measured Findings

Rats received 0.05 mg/kg psilocybin subcutaneously every other day over 21 days. Receptor measurements in medial prefrontal cortex used five animals per group at one and eight days after the final dose.

5-HT2A receptor **protein abundance** increased significantly at day eight, but not day one. The corresponding mRNA analysis found no statistically significant treatment effect; this does not prove that mRNA was identical across groups.

These findings did not demonstrate improvements in the assessed cognitive or sensorimotor outcomes. Molecular changes should not be presented as demonstrated behavioural benefit.

### Figure 4: Percentage and Measurement

[Figure 4 and caption](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-026-07132-6/figures/4) use the **day-one vehicle group as the 100% reference**. Protein measurements were normalised to GAPDH before presentation relative to the control reference.

The day-eight psilocybin bar visually reads about **270% of that reference**, approximately 2.7 times the reference or 170% above it. This is an approximate graphical reading, not an exact numerical mean supplied by the authors. Do not reproduce “280% increase” or treat “280% of control” as an exact reported result. Prefer “significantly increased” for short summaries.

### Interpretation and Untested Implications

**Homeostatic rebound after treatment cessation is a hypothesis.** The experiment did not establish that withdrawal caused the increase, demonstrate preceding receptor downregulation, or distinguish rebound from adaptations initiated during exposure. A nonsignificant mRNA result does not establish a specific post-transcriptional mechanism.

Protein abundance is not receptor occupancy, activation, sensitivity or proof of additional functional surface receptors. Plasticity-related molecular markers do not by themselves demonstrate new synapses, improved learning or therapeutic efficacy.

The authors discuss a possible recognition-memory effect, but the treatment did not improve the discrimination index. Within-group preference for a novel object is not evidence of superiority to vehicle.

These rat findings do not establish equivalent receptor changes, safety or benefit in human microdosing and do not validate a human dosing schedule.
