# Open Research Questions

## Purpose

This document describes how the Toolkit records and organises research questions that remain open or partially answered.

## Why open questions matter

Open questions help the Toolkit:

- identify gaps in current evidence
- prioritise future research and experiments
- maintain intellectual humility
- document where interpretation is still tentative

## Question structure

Each open research question can be described using the following elements:

- `id` — a stable short identifier
- `question` — a clear, answer-seeking statement
- `domain` — the relevant research area
- `current_evidence` — summary of what is known without inventing findings
- `key_gaps` — the main unknowns or uncertainties
- `relevant_sources` — links to references or research entries
- `related_frameworks` — toolkit modules or models connected to the question
- `suggested_methods` — potential approaches for addressing the question
- `notes` — additional context or definitions

## Example template

```markdown
id: question-2026-001
question: What are the key neural and phenomenological mechanisms that distinguish different altered states of consciousness?
domain: consciousness
current_evidence: Evidence exists for neural correlates of distinct states, but the full mapping of phenomenology remains incomplete.
key_gaps:
  - precise relationships between subjective reports and neural measures
  - consistency across different methods for inducing altered states
relevant_sources:
  - reference-id-001
  - reference-id-002
related_frameworks:
  - Consciousness & Human Potential
  - Interconnected Pyramids
suggested_methods:
  - mixed-methods phenomenology
  - multimodal neuroimaging
notes: This question is intended to guide inquiry rather than assert a definitive model.
```

## Organising open questions

- Group questions by theme, such as consciousness, wellbeing, practice, systems, or methodology.
- Track whether a question is currently active, partially addressed, or unresolved.
- Update the record when new evidence clarifies the question.

## Examples of open questions (without new findings)

- How can the Toolkit distinguish between evidence-based practice and promising but unproven methods?
- What are the most reliable ways to describe subjective experience while preserving scientific rigor?
- Which research questions are most important for connecting contemplative science with neuroscience?

## Maintaining the question list

- Encourage contributions of new questions with clear scope.
- Document unanswered questions as assets in the knowledge commons.
- Preserve the boundary between what is known and what remains to be explored.
