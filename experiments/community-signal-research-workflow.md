# Community Signal → Research Question Workflow

## Purpose

This workflow documents how community-sourced signals can inform the Toolkit's research process.
It is designed to treat community discussion, such as r/NeuronsToNirvana, as exploratory observational input rather than as clinical, causal, or confirmatory evidence.

## Scope

The workflow connects naturalistic observation to structured research questions while preserving methodological caution.
It is intended to integrate with Phase 2 systems for:

- research database structure
- evidence-rating methodology
- structured references
- research landscape categorisation

## Workflow Overview

Community observations
→ anecdotal reports
→ recurring patterns
→ candidate hypotheses
→ operationalised research questions
→ preregistered protocols
→ measured outcomes
→ analysis
→ replication
→ evidence update

## 1. Community observations

- Community platforms can surface informal ideas, experiences, practices and questions.
- In the Toolkit, these observations are recorded as exploratory input, not as established evidence.
- Example source: r/NeuronsToNirvana, which may reflect community reasoning, emerging interests, or naturalistic observations about consciousness and human potential.

## 2. Anecdotal reports

- Participants may share personal experiences, interpretations, or impressions.
- These reports may suggest areas worth investigating, but they are subject to strong biases and limitations.
- Treat anecdotal reports as stimulus material for further inquiry, not as evidence of causality.

## 3. Recurring patterns

- Look for patterns that appear across multiple independent reports.
- Patterns may indicate areas where the community is noticing similar phenomena or challenges.
- Recurring patterns are useful for generating candidate hypotheses, but they do not by themselves establish reliability.

## 4. Candidate hypotheses

- Transform recurring patterns into candidate hypotheses.
- Candidate hypotheses should be phrased clearly and tentatively.
- They are proposals for what might be true, not conclusions.
- Keep the distinction between observation and interpretation explicit.

## 5. Operationalised research questions

- Convert candidate hypotheses into operationalised research questions.
- Operationalised questions should specify the phenomenon of interest, the context, and the variables involved.
- These questions are suitable for structured research design and preregistration.

## 6. Preregistered protocols

- Preregistered protocols document the research plan before data collection begins.
- They should include the research question, hypothesis, key variables, outcome measures, analysis plan, and criteria for interpretation.
- Preregistration supports transparency and reduces the risk of post-hoc interpretation.

## 7. Measured outcomes

- Define measurable outcomes that align with the research question.
- Outcomes may be behavioural, experiential, physiological, or qualitative.
- Choose measures that are appropriate to the protocol and are described clearly.

## 8. Analysis

- Analyse the data according to the preregistered plan.
- For exploratory work, describe how observations and patterns will be summarised.
- For confirmatory work, use pre-specified criteria to evaluate hypotheses.
- Maintain transparency about any deviations from the original plan.

## 9. Replication

- Replication is critical for moving from exploratory observation toward evidence.
- If an initial protocol yields interesting patterns or tentative support, design follow-up studies that test the same research question with independent data.
- Replication may involve different participants, contexts, measures, or research teams.

## 10. Evidence update

- Update the research landscape and evidence records as new findings emerge.
- Distinguish between:
  - exploratory signals
  - emerging evidence
  - working hypotheses
  - speculative content
  - established evidence
- Use Phase 2 evidence-rating categories to classify new material consistently.

## Treating Community Material Appropriately

Community material should be treated as:

- exploratory observational input
- hypothesis-generation material
- a source of research questions and themes

Community material should not be treated as:

- clinical evidence
- causal proof
- confirmatory evidence
- a substitute for rigorously designed research

## Methodological Limitations

When using community-sourced signals, document the following limitations explicitly:

- self-selection bias
- reporting bias
- survivorship bias
- placebo / expectancy effects
- confounding
- regression to the mean
- community clustering
- duplicate or low-quality reports
- inability to establish causality
- privacy and ethical concerns

## Privacy and Ethical Notes

- Do not scrape personal data or expose identifying information.
- Respect community norms and platform terms of service.
- Protect individual privacy when documenting community signals.
- Use aggregated or anonymised summaries rather than quoting or reproducing identifiable content.

## Visualisations and community material

- Visuals drawn from community posts (images, diagrams, screenshots) are treated as exploratory signals only. Do not assume Reddit or other platform content is reusable; record provenance and permission status before embedding or redistributing.
- Follow `experiments/visualisation-protocol.md` when using any visual material derived from community sources. Prefer linking to original posts over embedding images; if embedding is necessary obtain explicit permission and document consent in the visual provenance record.

## Integration with Phase 2 systems

- Link workflow documents and observations to the research database framework.
- Use structured reference entries to record sources, even when sources are community materials.
- Apply evidence-rating methodology to any research claims generated from the workflow.
- Use the research landscape categories to situate findings and open questions.

## Documentation guidance

- Record community-signal workflows as part of the repository's research materials.
- When relevant, link to related research entries, references, and open questions.
- Keep the workflow distinct from confirmatory research documentation.
