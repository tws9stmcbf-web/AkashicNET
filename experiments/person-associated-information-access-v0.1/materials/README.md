## All enriched passages and final items

### F01: Marie Curie

Marie Curie investigated radioactivity through physics and chemistry. Born in Warsaw, she later worked in Paris. Research with Pierre Curie identified polonium and radium. The name polonium referred to the country of her birth. She later held a physics professorship at the Sorbonne. Her Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded in 1911. This passage concerns publicly documented research and professional life; it does not describe private memories or provide a hidden symbol.

Sources: S01, S02, S03, S13, S14. The final recall item asks: Which pair of elements did the passage associate with Curie's research? Key: Radium and polonium. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F02: C. V. Raman

C. V. Raman investigated the scattering of light. His work examined light after it interacted with matter. Some scattered light has a different frequency from the incoming light. This frequency change is associated with the Raman effect. The incoming light can have a single frequency while the scattered light includes shifted components. Raman received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930. This passage describes public observations about light, not private thoughts or a hidden symbol.

Sources: S04, S05, S15. The final recall item asks: Which property did the passage explicitly say differs between some scattered light and the incoming light? Key: The frequency of the light. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F03: Dorothy Crowfoot Hodgkin

Dorothy Crowfoot Hodgkin studied the structures of important biochemical molecules. Her work used X-ray crystallography to investigate how atoms were arranged. Molecules examined in her research included penicillin, vitamin B12 and insulin. These studies connected chemistry with the structures of substances relevant to biology. She received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1964. The passage describes publicly documented molecular research; it does not claim to reproduce her private experience or identify any hidden symbol.

Sources: S06, S16. The final recall item asks: Which vitamin did the passage name among Hodgkin's research subjects? Key: Vitamin B12. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F04: Barbara McClintock

Barbara McClintock studied genetics using maize plants. She followed inherited traits across successive generations. Her research showed that some genetic elements could move to different positions within chromosomes. Such movement can influence the expression of traits. These findings challenged the idea that genetic elements always remain in fixed positions. McClintock received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1983. This passage concerns public research findings, not access to her private thoughts or a hidden symbol.

Sources: S07, S17. The final recall item asks: Which crop did the passage associate with McClintock's research? Key: Maize. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F05: Niels Bohr

Niels Bohr worked on atomic structure and the radiation emitted by atoms. In 1913 he proposed a model of the hydrogen atom. In that model electrons occupy prescribed energy states, and a transition to a lower energy releases radiation. The model helped explain distinct wavelengths in atomic light. Bohr directed an institute for theoretical physics in Copenhagen. He received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1922. These are public scientific facts, not a record of private thoughts.

Sources: S08, S18, S19, S20. The final recall item asks: Which atom was the subject of the 1913 model mentioned in the passage? Key: Hydrogen. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F06: Rabindranath Tagore

Rabindranath Tagore wrote poetry, songs, stories and plays. He originally wrote in Bengali and later recast poetry in English, reaching additional readers. His work also addressed philosophy and social questions. In 1901 he founded an experimental school at Shantiniketan, aiming to bring Indian and Western learning traditions together. Tagore received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913. This passage describes public writing and educational activity; it does not reproduce private memories or reveal a hidden symbol.

Sources: S09, S21, S22. The final recall item asks: Which language did the passage say Tagore originally wrote in? Key: Bengali. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F07: Wangari Maathai

Wangari Maathai worked in Kenya on environmental protection and public life. She had academic training in biology. In 1977 she started the Green Belt Movement, encouraging women to plant trees in local communities. The movement responded to deforestation and linked ecological work with wider social concerns. Maathai received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004. This passage describes public activities and an organization; it does not establish contact with her or identify a hidden symbol.

Sources: S10, S23, S24, S25. The final recall item asks: What was the name of the movement identified in the passage? Key: Green Belt Movement. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

### F08: Santiago Ramón y Cajal

Santiago Ramón y Cajal investigated the structure of the nervous system. He used staining methods to make nerve cells visible for study. His account treated neurons as distinct cells that contact one another, rather than as one continuous network of cell material. He used and developed methods associated with Camillo Golgi. Cajal and Golgi shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1906. These public anatomical findings do not provide a readout of Cajal's memories.

Sources: S11, S12, S26. The final recall item asks: Who shared the 1906 prize with Cajal, according to the passage? Key: Camillo Golgi. Full distractors, the distinct award-year recognition item and the fictional inference item are in the JSON.

## Scoring and editorial rules

- Every final recall and inference item has four substantive options, exactly one keyed answer and an additional unscored do-not-know route. Store IDs, not display positions.
- Baseline free text and Day 0 open retrieval use the given rubric/examples, not fuzzy AI similarity. Independent raters freeze permitted synonyms and disagreement handling before data; no retrospective expansion to create successes.
- Delayed recognition tests award-year retention; final recall tests a different supplied fact. It is only a narrow manipulation check, not a measure of complete person knowledge or neural equivalence.
- Fictional inference keys follow their printed premises. They measure simple reasoning rather than historical information access, and are not facts attributed to the figure.
- Review recall distractors for plausibility and inference items for ceiling effects. Current items and workload matching have not been piloted or validated.
- Preserve all disagreements, item revisions and versions before freezing. After data collection begins, no replacement figures or item rewrites are allowed to rescue outcomes. A necessary correction becomes a reported deviation or a new registered version.
- Source review is independent only when someone other than the proponent/authoring assistant checks the facts and keys. This candidate has not received that review.
