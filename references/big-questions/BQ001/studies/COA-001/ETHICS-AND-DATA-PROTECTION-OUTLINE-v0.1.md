## Purpose and legal boundary

EDP-001 defines the minimum ethical and data-protection questions that every proposed COA-001 site must resolve before recruitment, device activation involving human-subject data, or access to identifiable clinical records.

It is an outline for institutional review, not legal advice, ethics approval, a consent form, a data-protection impact assessment or permission to conduct research. No provision in this document overrides applicable law, professional confidentiality, hospital policy, ethics-committee conditions or participant rights.

The site and sponsor must first determine whether the local implementation is classified as observational research, emergency research, a clinical trial, a medical-device investigation, another regulated study or more than one of these. The strictest applicable protections govern. Classification must be documented by the competent institutional and regulatory authorities; AkashicNET cannot self-classify or sponsor clinical activity.

Relevant high-level standards include:

- [WMA Declaration of Helsinki, 2024](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/);
- [ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf);
- where applicable, [EU General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng);
- where applicable to the classified study, [EU Clinical Trials Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/536/oj/eng);
- applicable national health-research, medical-device, hospital, confidentiality and deceased-person rules.

## Ethical premises

1. Emergency clinical care always takes priority over research.
2. The target system must not delay, obstruct, distract from or alter resuscitation.
3. Incapacity caused by cardiac arrest is a necessary characteristic of the proposed research population, not a convenience.
4. Inclusion without prior consent requires explicit lawful and ethics-approved authority; scientific interest alone is insufficient.
5. Later consent, assent and dissent are active safeguards, not administrative formalities.
6. Participants may report ordinary memories, dreams, no memory, distressing experiences or spiritual interpretations without authentication or dismissal.
7. No participant or family receives a claim that an account proves awareness outside the body, continuity, personal survival or an afterlife.
8. Death, incapacity, non-consent and withdrawal remain visible in the denominator ledger without forcing unlawful retention of identifiable data.
9. Research consent and the legal basis for processing personal data are distinct decisions.
10. Pseudonymized data remain personal data when re-identification is reasonably possible.
11. Identifiable narratives carry unusually high re-identification, dignity and reputational risk.
12. Positive, null and inconclusive results carry the same publication obligation, subject to privacy and rights controls.

Before any site opens, the following roles must be named in writing:

| Role | Minimum responsibility |
|---|---|
| Institutional sponsor | Overall legal, ethical, financial and quality responsibility |
| Principal investigator | Clinical/research conduct and participant protection |
| Treating clinical authority | Unrestricted priority over research activity |
| Research ethics committee/IRB | Independent approval, conditions and continuing oversight |
| Data controller(s) | Purposes, means, lawful basis, rights handling and accountability |
| Joint controllers, if any | Transparent allocation of controller duties |
| Data processor(s) | Processing only under documented instructions and contract |
| Data-protection officer/privacy office | DPIA, legal-basis, transfer and breach review |

| Independent safety/integrity body | Pause, adjudication and escalation authority |
| Participant-support contact | Questions, complaints, withdrawal and support pathways |

AkashicNET may maintain Stage-0 public methods. It must not receive identifiable or pseudonymous participant data, linkage keys, raw interviews, clinical records, staff rosters or operational secrets.

## Required jurisdiction-and-site annex

Each site must complete an approved annex before activation:

- country, region and institution;
- study classification and competent authority;
- applicable statutes, regulations and professional-confidentiality rules;
- sponsor and controller identities;
- Article 6 or equivalent lawful basis;
- Article 9 or equivalent special-category condition;
- emergency/deferred-consent authority;
- legally authorized representative/proxy rules;
- rules for deceased persons and post-mortem confidentiality;
- rules for participants who never regain capacity;
- age threshold and adult-only confirmation;
- medical-device and auditory-stimulus classification;
- ethics/IRB approval identifiers and conditions;
- prospective registration;
- insurance, indemnity, injury and compensation arrangements;
- complaints, ombudsman and supervisory-authority contacts;
- retention schedule;
- international-transfer mechanism;
- incident and breach-notification deadlines;
- approved participant-facing languages and accessibility arrangements.

A blank, ambiguous or disputed field keeps the site in `SITE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

## Research stages and consent authority

### A. Pre-event system operation

Commissioning, synthetic rehearsals and security testing should use no participant data.

A system may enter a clinical area only after engineering, infection-control, electrical, fire, human-factors and clinical-safety approval. It must not record patients, relatives, conversations, screens or staff by default.

Any pre-event collection of identifiable room, staff or patient information requires a separate approved purpose, lawful basis, minimization assessment and notice/waiver pathway.

### B. Emergency event before capacity

Because prospective consent is normally impossible during cardiac arrest, any research activity before capacity must fit a specifically approved emergency, deferred-consent, waiver or other lawful pathway.

The application must justify:

- why the research cannot be carried out with capable participants or delayed;
- why the population is scientifically necessary;
- why risks and burdens are minimal and proportionate;
- why clinical care is unaffected;
- exactly what begins before consent;
- what data are collected before consent;
- whether a legally authorized representative can practicably be consulted;
- how the participant or representative will be informed as soon as possible;
- what happens after refusal, death or continued incapacity;
- which minimum safety/audit records must be retained and under what authority.

No local team may infer authority from another jurisdiction’s approval.

### C. Recovery and approach

Only the treating team may decide when approach is clinically appropriate. INT-001 readiness, capacity, fatigue, distress, privacy and stop rules apply.

The approach must make clear:

- participation is voluntary and unrelated to clinical care;
- declining or withdrawing has no effect on treatment;
- no personal benefit or target match is promised;
- “no memory” is useful and acceptable;
- potentially distressing or spiritual content may arise;
- the interviewer cannot validate an afterlife or metaphysical interpretation;
- recording, transcription, linkage, retention and data sharing are optional only to the extent stated in the approved consent;
- the limits of withdrawal and deletion;
- whether general study results will be offered.

Consent must be documented by a trained person independent of any dependent clinical relationship where required. Capacity and continued willingness are reassessed throughout.

### INT-001 v0.2 contact and source boundaries

For the INT-001 v0.2 revision, primary and secondary contacts require separately recorded readiness and continuing willingness. A transcript lock does not authorise a later task. Recognition remains NOT AUTHORISED pending its coordinated review.

Approved consent information must not be withheld to protect blinding. Preserve the information-sheet/script version and any explanations as potential exposure. Necessary care, debrief and family contact must not be withheld or delayed for study purity.

Source locking protects integrity; it is not independent retention authority. The existing institutional withdrawal/deletion matrix governs original and secondary records. Preserve only audit metadata permitted under that authority. These statements add no jurisdiction-specific legal or consent approval.

### D. Legally authorized representative or proxy

Proxy/LAR involvement is permitted only when the applicable law and ethics approval authorize it.

The site must define:

- who qualifies and the order of priority;
- what decision standard applies;
- how known wishes and values of the participant are respected;
- whether the proxy can authorize interview, recording, linkage, follow-up and future use separately;
- how disagreement among representatives is handled;
- how participant assent and dissent are recognized;
- the deadline and method for participant re-consent after capacity returns.

Participant dissent overrides proxy permission unless the applicable authority explicitly determines otherwise. No interview proceeds merely because a proxy agreed when the participant resists or is distressed.

## Consent pathway matrix

Every eligible event receives one explicit pathway state:

| State | Research use boundary |
|---|---|
| `CAPABLE_CONSENT_OBTAINED` | Use only within approved consent and lawful-basis scope |
| `DEFERRED_CONSENT_PENDING` | Only minimum pre-approved emergency processing; no interview or discretionary secondary use |
| `LAR_PERMISSION_ACTIVE` | Only locally authorized activities; participant assent/dissent respected |
| `PARTICIPANT_RECONSENTED` | Continue under participant’s documented choices |
| `PARTICIPANT_DECLINED` | Stop prospective research; apply approved deletion/retention matrix |
| `PARTICIPANT_WITHDREW` | Stop future activity; apply stage-specific withdrawal matrix |
| `CAPACITY_NOT_REGAINED` | No participant interview; use only under explicit authority |
| `DIED_BEFORE_CONSENT` | Use only under explicit post-mortem ethics/legal authority |
| `REPRESENTATIVE_DECLINED` | Stop activities covered by representative decision |
| `NO_AUTHORIZED_PATHWAY` | No identifiable research use beyond legally required safety records |
| `CONSENT_STATUS_UNKNOWN` | Fail closed; hold and adjudicate |

Consent status must not change clinical eligibility. It is a flow state and cannot remove the eligible event from aggregate accounting.

## Death and never-consented cases

Death does not create blanket permission to use identifiable clinical information.

For participants who die before consent or never regain capacity, the approved annex must specify separately:

1. minimum device and safety data;
2. minimum denominator fields;
3. identifiable clinical data;
4. linkage to target exposure;
5. narrative or family-derived data;
6. future secondary use;
7. publication or case description.

The ethics committee, privacy office and applicable legal authority must decide what can be retained, pseudonymized, anonymized, aggregated or destroyed. National law, professional confidentiality, family interests and dignity may continue to apply even where a general data-protection regulation does not govern deceased persons.

Unless explicitly authorized, no family interview, case narrative, chart abstraction beyond minimum safety/denominator fields, or target-correspondence analysis is performed after death.

A lawful minimal aggregate denominator record may be retained to prevent survivorship and denominator distortion, but it must contain no more information than necessary and must not be presented as participant consent.

## Withdrawal and deletion matrix

Participant materials must explain withdrawal limits before consent. The site must freeze a matrix at least as protective as the following:

| Withdrawal point | Default action, subject to approved law and protocol |
|---|---|
| Before interview | No interview; stop new research collection |
| During interview | Stop immediately; retain or delete partial recording according to participant choice and approved safety/audit limits |
| After interview but before transcript lock | Stop new use; delete identifiable recording/transcript where legally and technically possible unless participant chooses retention |
| After transcript lock but before unblinding | Stop new activity; remove from discretionary analyses where possible; preserve only records required for audit, safety or lawful scientific integrity |
| After authorized unblinding/analysis lock | Stop future contact/use not already lawfully completed; prior analyses and immutable audit records may be retained only under the disclosed authority |
| After irreversible anonymization | Explain that identification and individual deletion may no longer be possible |
| After public aggregate reporting | Aggregate non-identifiable results normally cannot be retracted case by case |

Withdrawal of research participation does not necessarily equal withdrawal of the data-processing legal basis, and withdrawal of consent does not make prior lawful processing unlawful. The exact consequences depend on the selected lawful basis and must be stated in plain language.

No team may claim data are anonymous while retaining a linkage key or reasonably usable re-identification route.

## Data categories and minimization

| Data class | Examples | Default boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility/flow | age band, event area/time, compressions, survival, approach/consent states | Minimum necessary; complete denominator |
| Clinical timing | arrest, CPR, ROSC, medications, physiology | Prespecified fields only |
| Interview source | audio, transcript, interpreter content | Highest confidentiality; separate encrypted store |
| Phenomenology | structured responses and scales | Pseudonymous; avoid unnecessary diagnoses/beliefs |
| Contamination audit | conversations, media, room familiarity | Minimum content needed to test rival pathways |
| Target/device | assets, activation, render and clock logs | Separated from identity and interviews |
| Linkage | identity-to-study-ID map | Separate system, narrow custodial access |
| Staff/room data | staff IDs, presence, incident statements | Pseudonymous/minimized; staff notice and lawful basis |
| Security logs | account, access, export and incident events | No transcript content where metadata suffices |
| Public output | aggregate flow and results | Disclosure-control review; no raw narratives |

Free-text is collected only where scientifically necessary. Direct identifiers are prohibited from transcripts, scoring packets, analysis files and public artifacts.

## Audio, video and room-event recording

The default is **no audiovisual recording of resuscitation rooms**.

Independent display verification should prefer non-imaging technical sensors that cannot capture patients, screens, conversations or staff identities. If audiovisual room recording is proposed, it requires:

- separate scientific necessity and proportionality justification;
- explicit ethics and privacy approval;
- patient, staff, visitor and bystander analysis;
- applicable consent/waiver and notice mechanism;
- masking/redaction and restricted review;
- short, fixed retention;
- prohibition on reuse for training, publicity or unrelated surveillance;
- a dedicated DPIA and security test.

Research interview recording is governed separately by INT-001 and the approved participant consent.

## Auditory-channel safeguards

Before use, clinical and human-factors review must freeze:

- content, volume, duration and frequency;
- evidence that alarms, speech and resuscitation communication remain audible;
- placement and failure mode;
- staff audibility and contamination risks;
- stop authority;
- commissioning and periodic testing.

Any distraction, masking of clinical communication or staff confusion is a resuscitation-interference event and immediate pause condition. Auditory content must be neutral, non-distressing and culturally reviewed.

## Participant welfare and psychological safeguards

The study must plan for fatigue, delirium, impaired memory, trauma, fear, grief, spiritual meaning-making and family distress.

Required protections:

- clinically appropriate delay before approach;
- short sections with breaks and stop rules;
- continued assent;
- no repeated questioning to improve completeness;
- no correctness feedback during active collection;
- culturally and religiously respectful neutrality;
- no diagnosis or metaphysical interpretation by research staff;
- an approved referral pathway for clinical distress;
- an optional chaplaincy/spiritual-care pathway chosen by the participant, independent of scoring;
- bereavement-sensitive communication;
- no direct recruitment pressure from treating clinicians;
- documentation and review of interview-related adverse events.

Participants must not be told their account is false merely because it does not match a target, or true because it does.

## Incidental findings and target feedback

The protocol must define:

- what counts as an actionable clinical incidental finding;
- who verifies it;
- whether and how it is returned;
- emergency escalation;
- documentation and referral;
- non-return of uninterpretable research signals.

Researchers must not provide individual target-match feedback during collection. Whether any feedback is ever offered after study closure requires ethics approval, a validated score, psychological-risk review and a process that explains uncertainty. Metaphysical feedback is prohibited.

## Compensation, injury and complaints

Before recruitment, participant materials must state:

- reimbursement and compensation, if any;
- that payment is not contingent on memory content or completion;
- treatment and compensation arrangements for research-related harm;
- sponsor insurance or indemnity;
- independent complaint route;
- ethics-committee and data-protection contacts;
- protection against retaliation or effects on clinical care.

Payments must be reviewed for undue influence in a recently critically ill population.

## Data-processing legal basis

Where the GDPR applies, the controller must document both:

- an Article 6 lawful basis; and
- an Article 9 condition for health and other special-category data.

Consent to participate must not be treated automatically as GDPR consent. If consent is selected as a processing basis, it must satisfy the applicable standard and the consequences of withdrawal must be operationally feasible. If public-interest/scientific-research bases are selected, the specific Union or Member State law and safeguards must be named.

Article 89 or analogous research safeguards do not erase purpose limitation, minimization, security, transparency, rights analysis or accountability.

## Data-protection impact assessment

A site-specific DPIA is presumptively required before live processing because the design involves vulnerable emergency patients, health data, detailed narratives, linkage, novel monitoring/security systems and potentially multisite processing.

The DPIA must describe:

- purposes, necessity and proportionality;
- data subjects and data flows;
- controller/processor relationships;
- lawful basis and special-category condition;
- risks of re-identification, narrative disclosure, stigma, distress and family impact;
- insider, credential, transfer, device and inference threats;
- rights and consent workflows;
- minimization, pseudonymization, encryption and separation;
- retention/deletion;
- international transfers;
- incident response;
- residual risk and whether prior consultation with a supervisory authority is required.

No unresolved high residual risk may be accepted by the research team alone.

## Data-flow and access matrix

Before activation, a diagram and table must trace every data object from creation to destruction:

1. eligibility source;
2. pseudonymous event creation;
3. device/target logs;
4. identity linkage;
5. consent state;
6. interview recording;
7. transcript and contamination audit;
8. claim and scoring packets;
9. unblinding linkage;
10. analysis dataset;
11. archive;
12. aggregate publication;
13. deletion or irreversible anonymization.

For each transition record the sender, recipient, purpose, fields, security, location, legal basis, approval, retention and audit event.

Role access is least-privilege, time-limited and reviewed. Clinical staff do not receive target data; AkashicNET and public-site systems receive no participant-level data.

## Security minimums

- encryption in transit and at rest;
- institution-managed devices and accounts;
- multifactor authentication;
- role separation and periodic access review;
- separate linkage, transcript, target and analysis stores;
- key management, rotation, recovery and dual control;
- immutable or tamper-evident audit logs;
- controlled export and removable-media restrictions;
- secure deletion and backup-expiry verification;
- vulnerability, dependency and configuration management;
- tested backup/restore and incident exercises;
- processor due diligence and contracts;
- reproducible or measured software builds;
- no production data in development or AI services;
- no unapproved automated transcription or generative-model processing.

Security controls must be implemented and independently tested; specification alone is not validation.

## Retention and destruction

Before recruitment, the controller must approve a category-specific schedule covering:

- screening/eligibility log;
- consent records;
- identity linkage;
- clinical source extracts;
- interview recordings;
- transcripts and translations;
- contamination records;
- target/device/security logs;
- scoring packets;
- analysis datasets;
- safety and breach records;
- code, manifests and aggregate results;
- backups and disaster-recovery copies.

Each period requires a purpose, legal authority, start event, owner, deletion/anonymization method and exception process. “Keep indefinitely for research” is not an acceptable default.

Deletion must propagate to processors and scheduled backups, with exceptions documented. Irreversible anonymization requires a re-identification-risk assessment, not removal of names alone.

## International transfers and external services

No participant-level data leave the approved jurisdiction or institution until the controller documents:

- recipient and destination;
- transfer purpose and minimum fields;
- adequacy decision or other valid mechanism;
- contractual safeguards;
- transfer-impact and government-access assessment where required;
- encryption and key location;
- onward-transfer prohibition;
- audit, return and deletion rights.

Consumer cloud, personal storage, public repositories, unapproved transcription services and general-purpose AI systems are prohibited for participant-level data.

## Rights requests

The controller must provide an authenticated process for access, correction, restriction, objection, portability where applicable, erasure and complaint.

The response process must:

- avoid revealing other people’s information, target secrets or security details unnecessarily;
- distinguish source speech from later coding;
- preserve corrections as auditable amendments where research integrity requires;
- document any lawful research derogation and its necessity;
- meet jurisdictional deadlines;
- communicate the outcome in accessible language.

Research derogations are not automatic and must be tied to applicable law and safeguards.

## Incident and breach response

Every suspected loss, unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, re-identification, premature unblinding or integrity compromise triggers:

1. immediate containment without destroying evidence;
2. clinical-safety assessment;
3. preservation of logs and affected identifiers;
4. notification to sponsor, investigator, security and privacy leads;
5. classification of confidentiality, integrity, availability, blinding and participant risk;
6. independent assessment of analysis consequences;
7. regulatory, ethics and participant notification under applicable deadlines;
8. key/credential rotation and recovery;
9. root-cause and corrective/preventive action;
10. documented closure and protocol amendment where needed.

Where GDPR applies, the controller must assess supervisory-authority notification, including the Article 33 72-hour requirement where the legal threshold is met, and communication to affected people under Article 34 where required.

A breach is never hidden to protect a promising result.

## Public reporting and narrative protection

Public outputs are aggregate and disclosure-reviewed.

Prohibited without a separate, explicit, ethics-approved pathway:

- raw audio or transcripts;
- identifiable or readily recognizable narratives;
- exact dates/locations combined with unusual details;
- staff or family identities;
- case-level target assets and operational security details;
- quotations obtained under ordinary research consent but not public-quotation consent;
- promotional claims based on exceptional cases.

Separate consent for a quotation does not remove the duty to assess re-identification, family impact, withdrawal feasibility and scientific necessity. AkashicNET publication and website-promotion gates remain off.

## Required consultation and engagement

Before submission and again before Stage 2, the sponsor should document meaningful input from:

- cardiac-arrest survivors;
- bereaved family representatives;
- emergency and critical-care clinicians;
- nurses and resuscitation staff;
- disability, cognitive-access and language-access representatives;
- spiritual-care and secular-support representatives;
- privacy/data-protection specialists;
- research ethicists;
- independent statisticians and security reviewers.

Engagement informs design but cannot substitute for ethics approval or participant consent.

## Stage-0 and pre-recruitment gates

EDP-001 passes Stage 0 only when it is independently reviewed as a complete outline. It does not authorize recruitment.

Before any site recruits or processes live participant research data, all of the following must exist:

- jurisdiction/site annex complete;
- study classification confirmed;
- sponsor and institutional PI appointed;
- ethics/IRB approval;
- regulatory/device approvals where applicable;
- approved emergency/deferred-consent and re-consent pathway;
- approved deceased/never-consented data matrix;
- approved withdrawal/deletion matrix;
- controller/processor and lawful-basis record;
- DPIA with acceptable residual risk;
- data-flow/access matrix;
- fixed retention and transfer schedule;
- validated security and breach exercise;
- clinical device and auditory safety approval;
- participant materials and translations;
- compensation/injury/complaint pathway;
- prospective registration;
- staff certification and site rehearsal;
- independent statistical, clinical, ethics/privacy and security sign-off.

Any missing item leaves `RECRUITMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.
