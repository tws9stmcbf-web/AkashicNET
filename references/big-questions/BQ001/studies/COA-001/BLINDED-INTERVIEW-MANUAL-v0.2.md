## Purpose

INT-001 defines how a clinically stable survivor may be approached and interviewed after an eligible in-hospital cardiac-arrest event while reducing suggestion, information leakage, retrospective cueing and interviewer influence.

The interview measures a later report. It does not directly observe awareness during resuscitation. A report, target correspondence or striking narrative cannot by itself establish awareness, continuity, personal survival or an afterlife.

This manual does not authorize recruitment. Institutional clinical leadership, ethics/IRB approval, jurisdiction-specific consent authority, data-protection review, staff training and site agreements remain mandatory.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Clinical care always takes priority.
2. The treating team controls whether and when an approach is clinically appropriate.
3. Participation is voluntary and declining has no effect on care.
4. The interviewer remains blind to targets, target status, room-event logs and outcome-relevant clinical details.
5. Free narrative precedes structured questions, target prompts and recognition tasks.
6. The participant’s own language and temporal anchors are preserved.
7. Every known contamination exposure and blinding breach is recorded.
8. The first completed primary interview within the frozen window is designated by chronology, before scoring or target access. Failed locks and breaches remain attached to it; a later cleaner or more striking interview cannot replace it.
9. Lock the primary source recording, transcript and exposure audit before structured phenomenology, target-specific prompts or candidate presentation. Lock the primary claim set before any candidate access by its coders. Target processing and truth release require their separate authorised boundaries; an interview lock alone authorises neither.
10. Distress, fatigue, confusion or withdrawal stops the research interaction.
11. Positive, null, contradictory and non-scorable reports are retained.
12. No interviewer may tell a participant that an experience proves consciousness beyond the body or survival after death.

## Roles and separation

| Role | May know participant identity | May access interview content | May access targets/event logs before transcript lock |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Treating clinical team | Yes, as required for care | Only as clinically necessary | No study target access |
| Approach assessor | Minimum necessary | Readiness/consent fields only | No |
| Primary interviewer | Minimum necessary | Yes | No |
| Approved interpreter | Minimum necessary | Yes, during assigned interview | No |
| Transcript processor | Pseudonymous record only | Yes | No |
| Contamination auditor | Pseudonymous record and exposure log | Yes, after interview | No target identity |
| Target custodian | No participant identity | Transcript digest only before release | Encrypted/custodial access only |
| Primary scorer | No participant identity | Redacted locked transcript after L1 | No; masked candidates only in the later authorised scoring phase |
| Public/AkashicNET team | No | No raw interview or case-level data | No |

Participant-facing staff (including interpreters and support assistants) must not also serve as target/key custodians, candidate generators, primary scorers or truth-release approvers for the same participant-event. Interviewers must not code or adjudicate their own primary claims. A scorer must not act as the transcript-only claim coder for the same record. Pairwise role conflicts, credentials and access logs require independent review; a prohibition only on combining all three roles is insufficient.

Masked scoring occurs only in a later, separately authorised phase after primary transcript and claim-set locks. No scorer receives candidates before those locks. Clinical care may require event information; this manual restricts its research disclosure to blinded staff, never access needed for care.

## Interviewer eligibility and training

Before conducting any study interview, the interviewer must complete documented training in:

- neutral, non-leading interviewing;
- cardiac-arrest recovery, fatigue, delirium and memory limitations;
- trauma-informed and bereavement-sensitive communication;
- capacity and consent procedures approved for the jurisdiction;
- cultural and religious humility;
- interpreter use;
- contamination and exposure auditing;
- blinding protection and breach reporting;
- source-record handling, pseudonymization and transcript locking;
- participant distress and withdrawal procedures;
- rehearsal with synthetic cases and fidelity assessment.

Certification requires a prespecified passing score on recorded mock interviews assessed by two independent trainers. Retraining is required after a critical deviation or at the frozen interval.

## Preconditions for approach

An approach may occur only when all locally approved conditions are satisfied:

- the treating team confirms that contact will not interfere with care;
- the participant is sufficiently awake, medically stable and able to engage;
- capacity or the approved alternative consent pathway is documented;
- acute delirium, severe distress, uncontrolled pain or clinically significant fatigue does not make the interaction inappropriate;
- language access is available through a validated study language or approved interpreter procedure;
- no clinical staff member with target access participates in the approach;
- the interviewer’s blindness is confirmed immediately before contact.

The exact primary-interview window must be frozen after clinical and ethics review. Actual times since arrest, return of spontaneous circulation, regaining communicative capacity and every prior research/family debrief are recorded. Missing the preferred window does not erase the eligible event; it creates a flow state and reason.

## Consent and continued assent

The approved consent script must state plainly:

- the study concerns later memories and report correspondence during a medical emergency;
- no benefit is promised;
- the study does not assume that an afterlife, extracorporeal perception or continuity occurred;
- questions may be skipped;
- the interview may be paused or stopped at any time;
- the institutionally approved withdrawal, erasure and permitted-retention rules, including any genuine limits after anonymisation; transcript lock or unblinding alone must not be described as extinguishing participant rights;
- who may access recordings and transcripts;
- retention, transfer and deletion rules;
- how to complain or contact the institutional study team.

Consent is a continuing process. The interviewer must reconfirm willingness after any pause and before recognition tasks. Deferred, proxy or waiver pathways may be used only when explicitly approved for that jurisdiction and must define later re-consent.

## Readiness and stop rules

Before starting and at each section transition, record a brief approved readiness assessment covering:

- alertness and orientation;
- self-reported fatigue;
- pain or physical discomfort;
- emotional distress;
- desire to continue;
- clinician-requested limits.

The interviewer must pause or stop when:

- the participant asks to pause or stop;
- the treating team requests termination;
- distress, confusion, exhaustion or clinical deterioration crosses the frozen stop criterion;
- capacity becomes uncertain;
- privacy cannot be maintained;
- a critical blinding or contamination event occurs during the interview;
- the recording or identity-linkage system fails in a way that cannot be documented safely.

A stopped interview remains in the flow ledger with its reason. It is never restarted merely to obtain a more complete or favourable account.

## Pre-interview blinding declaration

Immediately before contact, the interviewer records:

- interviewer ID and certification version;
- confirmation of no target access;
- confirmation of no room-event-log access;
- clinical details already known and their source;
- any contact with clinical staff or family about the event;
- any accidental exposure to outcome-relevant information;
- device, recording and pseudonymous case-ID readiness;
- presence and role of every person in the room.

If blindness is uncertain or breached, hold the research approach and notify the independent integrity lead. Use a qualified unexposed replacement only under a prospectively approved rule, retaining the original exposure record. If the participant was already exposed, changing the interviewer does not remove that exposure. No local discretion may declare the record clean. In Stage 0, unresolved rules remain blocking design decisions.

## Interview sequence and protected source boundaries

This is a proposed sequence for a future approved protocol. In Stage 0 it may be examined using wholly fictional tabletop records only. No survivor approach, interview, live target task or participant-data processing is authorised.

The order is fixed. Stop for care, readiness, privacy or consent needs. Do not move a later task earlier to save time.

| Boundary | Content sealed | Access consequence |
|---|---|---|
| N0: free-narrative snapshot | Uninterrupted narrative and neutral continuers, with source offsets | Preserves exactly what preceded content-specific clarification; grants no target access |
| L1: `FINAL_PRIMARY_LOCKED` | Primary recording, verified transcript, temporal map, prompt/source labels, exposure audit, deviations and metadata | Ends primary recall collection; grants no candidate or truth access by itself |
| C1: `CLAIM_SET_LOCKED` | Independently coded and adjudicated primary claims under SDC-001 | Must precede candidate access in the scoring workflow |
| L2: `SECONDARY_SUPPLEMENT_LOCKED` | Any separately approved later structured interview, with a link to L1 | Never replaces L1 or adds primary claims |
| S1: scoring and analysis locks | Candidate manifest, independent/adjudicated scores and required analysis objects | Only then may separately authorised dual-control truth release occur under SDC-001/DFL-001 |

N0 is a proposed source-integrity checkpoint, not a new analysis population or a substitute for L1. Append source segments using a verified immutable manifest if a recording remains open; do not claim that a hash of an unfinished file locks future bytes. A missing checkpoint is explicit.

### Section 0: orientation and permission

Consent must be complete through the approved pathway before research questioning. Do not withhold material consent information to obtain a cleaner account. Record the exact information sheet, script version and any study explanations already given as potential exposure.

Proposed opening, subject to clinical, language and ethics review:

> I would like to ask what, if anything, you remember from the period around your medical emergency. Not remembering is a valid response. You may skip any question or stop at any time. Please use your own words, and tell me when you are unsure.

State the interviewer's actual knowledge limits accurately. Do not claim to know nothing if minimum clinical context was provided. Do not introduce near-death, extracorporeal, spiritual or target explanations merely to elicit them.

### Section 1: uninterrupted free narrative

> Please tell me what, if anything, you remember from around your medical emergency, starting wherever you wish.

Use silence and brief neutral continuers such as “What else, if anything, do you remember?” or “Please describe that in your own words.” Do not repeatedly ask after a clear no-memory response, complete sentences, list possible experiences or reward particular details.

Record no memory, fragments, uncertainty and contradictions verbatim. Close and timestamp N0 before later clarification. “No memory” is a valid report; refusal, inability to communicate and recording failure are different states.

### Section 2: participant-defined temporal map

Using only content already volunteered, ask:

> What makes you place that memory at that time?
>
> Is that order something you remember, something you worked out later, or are you unsure?

Allow no temporal anchor. Separate content, perceived order, claimed timing, confidence and the source of timing knowledge. Ask about last/first remembered moments without assuming that the participant experienced a loss or return of awareness.

Do not supply arrest, CPR, procedure or return-of-spontaneous-circulation details. A separate authorised clinical-data custodian may later align independently recorded times, with uncertainty, under a locked rule. The interviewer does not establish a physiological interval from narrative vividness.

### Section 3: participant-led clarification

> You used the words “[participant's exact words]”. Could you describe what you mean, without guessing?

Ask only what is needed to clarify the participant's words. Do not introduce absent modalities, a displaced viewpoint, target component categories or suggested equipment. If a modality checklist is scientifically necessary, place it in the secondary structured supplement and label its prompts.

Record spelling or spatial clarification without offering alternatives. Separate content confidence, temporal confidence and interpretation; use ordinary verbal responses until a confidence scale is frozen.

### Section 4: open environmental recall

> Is there anything else you remember about your surroundings at that time? It is fine to say no or unsure.

Avoid lists of images, colours, numbers or objects that reveal target composition. Any recalled sounds or surroundings remain eligible for later channel-specific coding; this question does not authenticate their origin.

For every new claim preserve exact words, first source span, eliciting prompt, claimed timing and its basis, uncertainty, and any volunteered information about later sources. Record whether first supplied in N0 or after a question. Do not collapse prompted and spontaneous statements.

### Section 5: contamination and exposure audit

Complete the structured audit below before L1. Start open:

> Since the emergency, what, if anything, have you been told, seen, read or discussed about what happened?
>
> Is there anything you are unsure whether you remember yourself or learned afterwards?

Then cover the fixed source categories without naming actual target features or known room events. Ask once, neutrally; no pressure to prove the account independent. “Unknown” remains unknown.

New substantive recollections first introduced in this audit receive `AUDIT_ELICITED` source labels. They cannot be relabelled spontaneous. Their permitted scoring contribution must be frozen before recruitment.

### Section 6: primary closing

> Is there anything you would like to add or correct about what you have told me? You may also leave it as it is.

Record additions and corrections with their source offsets and elicitation labels. Preserve earlier contradictory wording. Do not erase a participant's correction, nor silently rewrite the earlier account.

Reconfirm permitted data use, willingness to finish and support needs. Explain that the interviewer cannot give target or event-match feedback. End primary collection and complete L1 using the lock procedure below. If this cannot be done reliably, later prompting is held.

### Section 7: secondary structured supplement, after L1 only

Structured phenomenology, modality/viewpoint checklists and any generic target-directed recall belong to a separately labelled supplement. They may be undertaken only under the later approved protocol, continuing consent, readiness and burden limits. Instruments require appraisal, language suitability and study-use rights. Do not reproduce unlicensed scales here.

A possible target-directed prompt for review is:

> Do you remember anything else that you think may be relevant? Please do not guess; no memory and unsure are valid answers.

If reviewers require a prompt explicitly mentioning presentation of an image or sound, freeze that wording and record it as target-concept exposure. Such responses are prompted secondary data even if no candidate was displayed. Target presence, activation, contents and sequence remain concealed.

Record a new session ID, start time, script/instrument version, all intervening exposures and a link to L1. Lock the supplement independently as L2. Later retellings, newly elaborated detail or repetitions never add independent primary observations. Do not score the same content twice.

### Section 8: recognition task, BLOCKED pending coordinated design

Recognition is retained as a proposed research component, but **this revision does not authorise any candidate presentation**. TS-001 requires primary transcript lock before target-package access; DFL-001 forbids the masked candidate set entering the interview zone and requires further locks before truth release. The existing interview's single-session order does not resolve these boundaries.

Before even a future approved participant recognition task is enabled, a coordinated TS-001/DFL-001/SDC-001/EAP-001/SAP-001 amendment must:

1. distinguish automated restricted target processing, candidate exposure, truth-position disclosure and final analysis unblinding;
2. specify the institutional system and separate recognition administrator, who remains blind to truth position and has no role in primary interviewing or claim extraction;
3. require verified L1, C1 and any intended pre-recognition L2, completed source audits, valid data-use authority and no unresolved critical incident;
4. define a permitted recognition-zone transfer, whose scope cannot override the existing prohibited flow by inference;
5. construct foils without narrative access, commit the packet/order and test appearance, metadata, loading, accessibility and response cues;
6. freeze visual and auditory task ordering, number of trials, response options, missing/unsure handling, confidence collection, timing and multiplicity;
7. prevent recognition records or later recollections reaching primary claim coders/scorers, and record the earliest candidate exposure as irreversible for later recall;
8. lock recognition responses separately before any truth feedback or truth-linked analysis;
9. receive independent methods, security, statistical, clinical and ethics review.

Until then set `RECOGNITION_NOT_AUTHORISED`, not “completed”, “negative” or “missing response”. Do not remove recognition from the study's intended outcomes silently or substitute it into the primary endpoint. Its final analytic role remains with the coordinated SAP.

Visual and auditory tasks remain separate. An auditory correspondence alone cannot establish extracorporeal perception. The interviewer provides no correctness feedback at any stage.

## Contamination and exposure audit

Blinding, exposure and report content are separate dimensions. A sincere account can have an ordinary information pathway. A belief, religion or unusual experience alone is not evidence of contamination.

| Source category | Neutral coverage | Record required |
|---|---|---|
| Care/debrief | What explanations about the emergency were given? | By whom, when, approximate words, before/after first recollection |
| Family/visitors/other patients | What was discussed or shown? | People present, content and opportunities; do not collect unnecessary identities |
| Prior research/consent | What study information, questions or choices were encountered? | Script/material version and first exposure time |
| Records/screens/media | Any notes, messages, images, recordings or searches consulted? | Content and source, with participant-reported versus independently verified status |
| Room familiarity/ordinary access | Any earlier or later familiarity with those surroundings? | Route, timing and uncertainty; no investigator-supplied room detail |
| Interpreters/support persons | Did anyone explain, suggest or supplement an answer? | Original words, translation/intervention, source offsets |
| Interpretation/advice | Did any discussion influence how the experience was described? | Specific external suggestion if reported; beliefs alone remain context |
| Research task exposure | Any structured item, target concept or candidate previously encountered? | Exact prompt/task and exposure boundary, including accidental presentation |

For each exposure collect: pseudonymous exposure ID; source type; exposure time or bounded interval with uncertainty; exact or approximate content labelled as such; who received it (participant/interviewer/interpreter/coder); first known recollection time; report time; related claim IDs where available; plausible route; confirmation source; known/possible/unknown status; action and adjudication reference.

Do not infer that a memory preceded exposure merely because the participant says it felt earlier. Preserve the report and its evidential basis separately. An audit completed with explicit unknown answers is procedurally complete but does not establish absence of contamination.

Independent collateral checks, if later authorised, use a separate auditor and sealed records. They cannot feed event details back to participant-facing staff or add claims to the primary transcript. Do not delay or restrict necessary care, family contact or clinical explanation to protect study blinding.

## Contamination and breach decisions

| Finding | Immediate research action | Record and analytic boundary |
|---|---|---|
| No known exposure after completed audit | Preserve audit and access evidence | `NO_KNOWN_CONTAMINATION` means not detected, not proven absent |
| Possible/unknown source or incomplete audit | Hold clean-status classification; seek authorised independent review | Retain event and unknown fields; no automatic per-protocol acceptance |
| Interviewer/interpreter learns activation, target or relevant event details | Pause affected interaction; preserve source and incident record | Critical/uncertain blinding state; replacement does not erase the breach |
| Participant receives relevant information | Record earliest exposure and affected claims/channels | Do not discard the entire record by discretion; apply frozen channel-specific rules |
| Candidate presented before L1/C1 | Stop research task, contain access, notify integrity lead | Critical ordering incident; no retrospective lock can cure it |
| Information first appears after L1 | Create linked secondary/addendum record | Cannot modify primary transcript, claim set or score |
| Post-lock evidence invalidates an apparently clean record | Append incident and adjudication; pause interpretation | Preserve original frozen score; report corrected eligibility/validity and required sensitivity consequences |

Critical incidents trigger the programme pause rules in EAP-001/DFL-001. A numerical tolerance for interviewer breaches does not override zero-tolerance premature target access. Unknown blindness counts as breach pending adjudication under EAP-001. Every exclusion applies only to its frozen analysis population; eligible events and appropriate broad analyses retain their flow records, subject to lawful retention.

## Prohibited interviewer behaviours

The interviewer must not:

- introduce a displaced viewpoint or an extracorporeal interpretation before the participant volunteers it;
- ask “Did you see the hidden target?” or any question that presumes target visibility or exposure;
- describe target appearance, frequency or location;
- reveal whether the device activated;
- disclose staff actions or room events;
- praise a “hit” or express disappointment at a null report;
- use spiritual, religious, materialist or paranormal interpretations as fact;
- press for detail after uncertainty, distress or refusal;
- combine the participant’s words with later interpretations in the transcript;
- correct contradictions during the interview;
- invite family or staff to supplement the account before lock;
- conduct informal outcome-focused conversations outside the recorded procedure.

## Interview environment

The site must provide:

- auditory and visual privacy appropriate to clinical care;
- minimal avoidable interruption;
- no visible target-system information;
- documented identities and roles of everyone present;
- a secure approved recording system;
- no personal device recording;
- a plan for emergency clinical interruption;
- accessible communication aids that do not introduce target content.

Clinical interruptions are timestamped. The interview resumes only after readiness and continued assent are reconfirmed.

## Interpreter procedure

An interpreter must:

- be institutionally approved and confidentiality-bound;
- receive study-neutrality and blinding training;
- have no target or event-log access;
- translate in the first person as literally as practical;
- avoid explanation, summarization or cultural interpretation during data collection;
- identify unavoidable clarifications;
- complete a post-interview fidelity declaration.

The original-language recording and approved translated transcript are both retained. Translation disagreements are resolved without opening target records.

## Source recording and transcript production

- Record the complete interaction from the approved opening through closing, including pauses and interruptions.
- Preserve the original source file read-only.
- Create a pseudonymous working copy for transcription.
- Mark inaudible passages, overlaps, gestures and interpreter interventions explicitly.
- Preserve grammatical errors, uncertainty and repetition where they affect meaning.
- Separate verbatim speech from interviewer notes and later coding.
- Record software, version, human corrections and processor identity.
- Automatic transcription may assist but never replaces human verification under the approved privacy controls.

## Transcript finalization and lock

L1 occurs after primary closing and before the secondary structured supplement. Before any permitted downstream target processing:

1. verify the source recording’s integrity;
2. complete the verbatim transcript;
3. link every correction to an audit entry;
4. finish the contamination/exposure audit;
5. confirm participant and event linkage;
6. mark the transcript `FINAL_PRIMARY_LOCKED`;
7. calculate digests for source recording, transcript and interview metadata;
8. obtain the required independent timestamp or repository receipt;
9. record lock time, protocol version and responsible roles;
10. prohibit replacement or silent editing.

Information discovered after lock creates an append-only amendment linked to the original digest. It cannot silently alter the primary transcript, claim set or primary score. A material integrity problem may invalidate an interpretation or require a clearly labelled sensitivity analysis; immutable originals do not justify retaining a false clean-status claim.

Verify that receipts bind exact bytes, protocol/manual version, event/session ID and all constituent objects. Record lock and release timestamps with clock source and uncertainty. If ordering cannot be established, mark it uncertain and hold release. Store receipts in the approved institutional environment, never public repositories containing participant-linked material. Independent verification failure leaves `TRANSCRIPT_LOCK_INCOMPLETE`.

Locking protects integrity; it does not create authority to retain data against applicable withdrawal or erasure requirements. The institutional controller must govern deletion or restricted retention, preserving only permitted audit metadata. No new retention rule is established by this draft.

## Blinding and contamination states

Every primary interview receives all applicable states:

- `INTERVIEWER_BLIND_CONFIRMED`
- `INTERVIEWER_BLIND_UNCERTAIN`
- `INTERVIEWER_CRITICAL_UNBLINDING`
- `NO_KNOWN_CONTAMINATION`
- `CONTAMINATION_POSSIBLE`
- `CONTAMINATION_CONFIRMED`
- `CONTAMINATION_AUDIT_INCOMPLETE`
- `TRANSCRIPT_LOCKED_BEFORE_UNBLINDING`
- `PREMATURE_UNBLINDING`
- `PRIMARY_INTERVIEW_INCOMPLETE`
- `PARTICIPANT_WITHDREW`
- `CLINICAL_STOP`

Unknown states remain unknown; they are not converted to clean records.

Store blinding status, exposure status, audit completion, lock ordering and interview disposition as separate fields. Within each field, current states must be mutually exclusive: a record cannot simultaneously be blind-confirmed and blind-uncertain, or have confirmed contamination and no known contamination. Preserve status transitions and their evidence as history. New proposed states include `NARRATIVE_SNAPSHOT_SEALED`, `SECONDARY_SUPPLEMENT_LOCKED` and `RECOGNITION_NOT_AUTHORISED`; schema validation and compatibility mapping remain pending. These labels describe required records, not implemented controls.

## Fidelity monitoring

A prespecified sample of interviews from every interviewer and site is independently reviewed for:

- section order;
- neutrality;
- prohibited prompts;
- free-narrative completeness;
- temporal-source separation;
- recognition timing;
- contamination audit completeness;
- distress/stop-rule compliance;
- recording and transcript integrity.

Critical deviations trigger immediate pause, retraining and independent adjudication. Fidelity results are reported by site and interviewer using pseudonymous IDs.

The locked interview metadata must include:

- pseudonymous event and participant IDs;
- site and protocol version;
- interviewer and interpreter certification versions;
- approach, consent, start, stop and lock timestamps;
- readiness checks and pauses;
- persons present;
- primary/secondary interview status;
- section completion states;
- recording and transcript digests;
- blinding states;
- contamination exposures;
- distress, withdrawal and clinical-stop states;
- deviations and amendments;
- eligibility for each EAP-001 analysis population;
- unblinding authorization status;
- N0 source boundary and L1/C1/L2 references where applicable;
- first-capture and first-exposure timestamps with uncertainty;
- per-claim elicitation labels and original source spans;
- recording, transcription and translation lineage;
- separate interview completion, lock success and primary designation fields;
- recognition authorisation state, kept distinct from participant response.

The record contains no target identity before authorized release.
