## Does consciousness continue beyond the individual?

## Evidence legend

- **Established Evidence** — appropriately reviewed support with explicit provenance.
- **Interpretation** — reasoned reading or synthesis, presented as interpretation.
- **Lived Experience/Testimony** — first-person or reported experience, preserved without universalising it.
- **Hypothesis** — specific investigable proposition that remains unconfirmed.
- **Speculation** — possibility with insufficient support for stronger classification.

## 1. What exactly is being asked?

Define the operational meanings of **consciousness**, **individual**, **continuity**, **death**, **memory** and **identity** before evaluating claims. Different definitions can produce apparently conflicting answers.

## 4. Investigation domains

### Neuroscience & consciousness models
Evidence concerning neural dependence, perturbation, loss/recovery of consciousness and competing theories.

### Cardiac arrest & near-death experiences
Prospective studies, phenomenology, veridical-perception claims, controls, timing limitations, replication and alternative explanations.

### Memory, identity & continuity of self
What would have to persist for continuity to count as continuity of an individual rather than information, influence or resemblance?

### Reincarnation-case research
Case methods, documentation, prospective versus retrospective evidence, information leakage, cultural effects, replication and methodological critiques.

### Contemplative, philosophical & wisdom traditions
Preserve traditions as historically and philosophically important sources without automatically converting longevity or authority into empirical proof.

### Alternative and unresolved models
Represent testable hypotheses separately from broader metaphysical speculation.

## 5. Contradictions

Conflicting evidence is displayed rather than averaged away. Record what conflicts, whether the studies test the same proposition, and what evidence could resolve the disagreement.

## 6. Lived experience

Experiences can be meaningful data about human phenomenology. They are not automatically universal evidence about external ontology.

## 7. What remains unknown?

- Which anomalous reports survive prospective controls and independent replication?
- How should identity be operationalised across discontinuity?
- Which apparent disagreements are actually disagreements about definitions?
