# BIG QUESTION #001

## Does consciousness continue beyond the individual?

**Current AkashicNET status: UNRESOLVED**

AkashicNET does not begin this investigation with a preferred metaphysical answer. The current evidence map contains neuroscience, cardiac-arrest/NDE research, memory and identity research, reincarnation-type case literature, contemplative frameworks and philosophy of mind. These sources do not all answer the same question, and they must not be collapsed into a single score.

## Evidence legend

- **Established Evidence** — a scoped observation supported by the cited research record.
- **Interpretation** — a reasoned synthesis, framework or philosophical reading of evidence.
- **Lived Experience/Testimony** — reported experience preserved as testimony rather than universal proof.
- **Hypothesis** — a specific proposition that remains unconfirmed.
- **Speculation** — a possibility with insufficient support for stronger classification.

## What we know

### Brain states and conscious states are strongly related under studied conditions

**Established Evidence.** Experimental and clinical research supports strong relationships between brain state, conscious state, autobiographical memory and experienced self-continuity. Anesthetic studies show measurable changes associated with loss or alteration of consciousness, while memory research links autobiographical memory to subjective continuity of self.

**Boundary:** this is evidence for dependence under the conditions studied. It does **not** by itself demonstrate that personal consciousness cannot continue after irreversible death.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-MASCHKE-2024-OBS-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-ELHAJ-2019-OBS-01`.

### Some resuscitated patients report experiences associated with cardiac arrest

**Established Evidence.** AWARE and AWARE II prospectively studied cardiac-arrest populations and documented survivor reports of remembered experiences. AWARE 2014 reported a small proportion of interviewed survivors describing explicit visual or auditory recall related to resuscitation, including one case reported as containing verifiable event details.

**Boundary:** cardiac arrest during attempted resuscitation is not equivalent to irreversible biological death. Retrospective recall after recovery does not by itself establish the exact neurophysiological timing of the experience.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-AWARE-2014-OBS-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-AWARE-2014-OBS-02`; `CLAIM-BQ001-AWARE2-2023-OBS-01`.

### Near-death inference has important methodological constraints

**Interpretation.** Neuroscientific commentary and review literature emphasizes careful distinctions among cardiac arrest, brain death, unresponsiveness and consciousness. Contemporary models propose converging neurophysiological mechanisms that may account for major NDE features.

**Boundary:** a plausible neuroscientific model is not complete proof that every NDE has been mechanistically explained. Methodological criticism is not proof that consciousness cannot survive death.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-MARTIAL-2022-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-MARTIAL-2025-INTERP-01`.

### Psychological self-continuity is not the same thing as metaphysical identity

**Interpretation.** Contemporary psychology treats self-continuity as a subjective connection among past, present and future selves. Autobiographical memory appears to contribute to this experienced continuity.

**Boundary:** a psychological sense of continuity is not identical to numerical or metaphysical personal identity, and these studies do not test post-mortem persistence.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-SOW-2022-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-SEDIKIDES-2023-INTERP-01`.

### A peer-reviewed literature exists on children reporting previous-life memories

**Established Evidence.** A 2021 scoping review identified a research literature dominated by observational studies, case reports, interviews and documentary analysis. Comparative psychological studies and individual case reports document unusual reports and, in some cases, biographical correspondences.

**Boundary:** the existence of a matching case history is not proof that reincarnation caused it. Retrospective recall, family information transfer, selective reporting, publication bias, witness dependence and documentation timing remain important methodological risks.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-SCOPING-2021-OBS-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-HARALDSSON-2003-OBS-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-MORAES-2024-OBS-01`.

### Stronger reincarnation-type claims require stronger controls

**Interpretation.** Stronger inference would require early documentation, explicit accounting of information pathways, separated witnesses, prospective or blinded verification where feasible, and transparent reporting of both matches and misses.

**Boundary:** failure of one psychological explanation does not establish reincarnation. Conversely, psychological correlates do not establish that reincarnation is impossible.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-METHODS-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-TUCKER-2025-INTERP-01`.

### Contemplative traditions broaden the models of selfhood under discussion

**Interpretation.** Buddhist non-self analyses and modern meditation frameworks describe ways in which the ordinary sense of self can be reduced, reconstructed or deconstructed. These traditions and frameworks are relevant to how humans conceptualize selfhood and experience.

**Boundary:** contemplative phenomenology, spiritual authority, historical longevity and cross-tradition similarity do not establish an external metaphysical ontology or prove survival after death.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-SIDERITS-2011-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-DAHL-2015-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-VAGO-2012-INTERP-01`.

### Philosophy leaves more than one ontology conceptually open

**Interpretation.** Physicalism, dualism and panpsychism remain major philosophical frameworks relevant to consciousness. Physicalism emphasizes physical dependence; dualism allows mind and physical reality to be fundamentally distinct in some sense; panpsychism treats mentality or experience as fundamental or widespread.

**Boundary:** philosophical coherence is not empirical proof. Dualism or panpsychism does not demonstrate personal survival, and physicalism as a philosophical position is not itself experimental proof of non-survival.

Traceability: `CLAIM-BQ001-PHYSICALISM-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-DUALISM-INTERP-01`; `CLAIM-BQ001-PANPSYCHISM-INTERP-01`.

## Competing models

### Biological-dependence model

Individual conscious experience appears strongly dependent on functioning biological systems under observed conditions. This model is supported by neuroscience, anesthesia, memory and clinical evidence.

**Status: UNRESOLVED with respect to post-mortem continuity.** Evidence of dependence in living or resuscitated humans does not logically prove that continuation is impossible.

### Continuity / survival model

Some anomalous reports, reincarnation-type cases and non-reductive philosophical positions leave open the possibility that some aspect of consciousness could continue beyond individual biological functioning.

**Status: UNRESOLVED.** Anomalous reports and conceptual possibility do not establish that continuation actually occurs.

## Contradictions and tensions

The evidence map contains a genuine tension. On one side, conscious experience, memory and self-processing are repeatedly associated with measurable brain function. On the other, some cardiac-arrest reports and reincarnation-type cases contain observations that investigators regard as anomalous or difficult to explain with simple accounts. These are not symmetrical forms of evidence, and neither side is permitted to win by source count, popularity or model-support edges.

A useful investigation therefore asks which observations could discriminate between models rather than asking which side has accumulated more suggestive material.

## What this evidence does **not** show

It does not show that cardiac arrest equals irreversible death. It does not show that an NDE proves brain-independent consciousness. It does not show that a neuroscientific model proves all NDEs are fully explained. It does not show that memory dependence proves non-survival. It does not show that a matching past-life case proves reincarnation. It does not show that failure of a simple psychological account proves reincarnation. It does not show that meditation validates a metaphysical doctrine. It does not show that physicalism, dualism or panpsychism has experimentally settled the question.

## Open questions

1. Which observations could genuinely discriminate biological-dependence models from continuity models?
2. Can prospective cardiac-arrest studies establish tighter temporal links between reported experience and measured brain state?
3. Can reincarnation-type research produce prospectively documented and independently verified cases with information pathways tightly controlled?
4. What would count as continuity of an *individual* rather than continuity of information, resemblance, influence or cultural transmission?
5. Which philosophy-of-mind claims can be translated into empirically discriminating predictions?

## Current conclusion

**UNRESOLVED.**

The current evidence strongly supports relationships between biological brain states, memory and conscious experience. It also documents anomalous reports and contested case literatures whose strongest interpretations remain methodologically and conceptually disputed. Across the four validated evidence batches, there is not yet sufficient evidence to establish either that personal consciousness survives irreversible biological death or that such survival is impossible.

That is not a failure to answer the question. It is the present evidence boundary.
