# BQ001 — Does consciousness continue beyond irreversible biological death?

**Current status: UNRESOLVED**

This public synthesis is a map of the four validated BQ001 evidence batches. It does not add a new metaphysical conclusion. Every substantive section is linked in `public-synthesis-v0.1.json` to existing validated claim IDs and source IDs.

## What we know

Under studied conditions, conscious state is measurably related to brain state. Research on anesthesia and neural correlates supports strong brain–conscious-state relationships without showing that every possible form of consciousness is reducible to those tested conditions.

Some cardiac-arrest survivors report memories or perceptions associated with resuscitation. Prospective studies preserve important denominators and methodological limits: survivor and interview selection are substantial, recall after recovery does not uniquely establish when an experience occurred, and cardiac arrest under resuscitation is not the same state as irreversible biological death.

Autobiographical memory is associated with subjective self-continuity in psychological research. That is evidence about experienced continuity of self, not proof that autobiographical memory defines metaphysical personal identity.

A peer-reviewed observational literature also exists around children reporting previous-life memories. It includes case reports, interviews, documentary comparisons and some psychological comparison studies. The literature contains reported correspondences and anomalies, but it is methodologically heterogeneous and does not establish reincarnation as a proven mechanism.

## Competing models

The evidence is compatible with multiple interpretive frameworks. Physicalist approaches emphasize the dependence of mind on physical processes. Dualist approaches allow mind and physical processes to be fundamentally distinct in some respect. Panpsychist approaches treat mentality or experience as fundamental or widespread in nature.

These are philosophical frameworks, not empirical verdicts. Likewise, AkashicNET model-support edges are not votes. A claim linked to a model does not become stronger evidence merely because it supports that model.

## Evidence by domain

**Neuroscience and cardiac arrest:** brain-state/conscious-state relationships are experimentally measurable; resuscitation research contains reported experiences and physiological observations but substantial timing and selection constraints.

**Memory and self-continuity:** autobiographical memory and subjective continuity are related under studied psychological and clinical conditions, while metaphysical identity remains a separate question.

**Reincarnation-type cases:** a published observational literature exists, including reported biographical correspondences and psychological comparisons. Stronger inference requires better control of information leakage, documentation timing, witness dependence, selective reporting and alternative explanations.

**Contemplative traditions:** Buddhist analyses of non-self and scientific frameworks for meditation can clarify how the experienced self may be constructed or transformed. They do not by themselves demonstrate what happens after death.

**Philosophy of mind:** physicalism, dualism and panpsychism clarify different possible ontologies. Philosophical coherence alone does not establish survival or non-survival.

## Contradictions and limits

Several distinctions are essential. Cardiac arrest is not irreversible biological death. Retrospective recall is not a timestamp for the underlying experience. Subjective self-continuity is not identical to numerical or metaphysical identity. A correct or apparently anomalous case match does not uniquely identify its cause. A plausible neuroscientific model is not automatically a complete explanation. A contemplative teaching or philosophical position is not empirical proof.

## What this does not show

Evidence for brain dependence under tested conditions does **not** prove that consciousness cannot continue after irreversible biological death.

Reports around resuscitation, anomalous memories or reincarnation-type cases do **not** prove that consciousness survives death or that reincarnation occurs.

Changes in self-processing during meditation do **not** establish metaphysical non-self, brain independence or post-mortem survival.

Physicalism does not become proven simply because neuroscience finds brain–conscious-state relationships, and dualism or panpsychism does not become proven because some observations remain unexplained.

## Open questions

Important unresolved questions include the timing and neurophysiological substrate of reported resuscitation experiences; the explanatory limits of current neuroscientific NDE models; whether stronger prospective methods can substantially reduce information-leakage and reconstruction risks in reincarnation-type research; and how empirical findings should constrain competing philosophies of mind.

## Current conclusion

**UNRESOLVED.** The present evidence is strong enough to rule out simplistic claims of proof on either side, but not strong enough to select a final metaphysical answer.

AkashicNET therefore preserves the uncertainty rather than converting evidence, testimony, interpretation, philosophy or model preference into certainty.
