# 📚 References

A structured collection of research papers, books, datasets, historical sources and other materials relevant to the Noetic Sciences Toolkit.

## 🎯 Purpose

References provide the evidence trail behind the Toolkit's frameworks, experiments and educational materials.

The goal is to make claims **traceable, verifiable and open to critical review**.

## 🔬 Preferred Sources

Where available, prioritise:

- Peer-reviewed research
- Systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- Primary research
- Academic books
- Established research databases
- Official institutional sources
- Historical primary sources

## 🧭 Community Sources & Provenance

Some Toolkit materials draw on community discourse, symbolic frameworks, or lived-experience narratives as hypothesis-generating inputs. These sources are recorded as community/conceptual material and are kept separate from formal evidence.

- [Project Akashic Library / Codex Nexus](community/akashic-library-codex-nexus.md)
- [One Source, Many Paths](community/one-source-many-paths.md)

These entries preserve the exact original URLs, attribution, and provenance, and they explicitly note that they are not scientific evidence.

## ⚖️ Evidence Status

Where useful, sources can be labelled:

- **Established**
- **Emerging**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Speculative**
- **Historical / Cultural**

These labels describe the status of the *claim or interpretation*, not the intrinsic value of the source.

## 🔗 Citation Record

A useful reference entry should include, where available:

- Author(s)
- Title
- Year
- Publication
- DOI or stable URL
- Research area
- Evidence status
- Brief relevance to the Toolkit

## 🧐 Critical Reading

A citation does not automatically validate a claim.

When adding a source, ask:

> **What does this source actually demonstrate?**

Also consider methodology, sample size, limitations, replication and alternative interpretations.

## 🌱 Living Bibliography

This collection will evolve as new research and sources are identified.

Contributors are encouraged to add both **supporting and contradictory evidence**.

> **A trustworthy knowledge commons should make it easy to discover evidence that challenges its own assumptions.**
