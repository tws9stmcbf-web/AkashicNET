## Appraisal of search reproducibility and selection

**Strengths.** A broad database set, declared eligibility boundaries, an accessible search supplement and multiple eligibility assessors make the review useful for discovery. Spontaneous reports and hypnosis-induced narratives warrant separate questions and controls. Excluding the latter helps define the target population; it cannot validate the former. Independent eligibility assessment reduces one review-level source of selection error; it does not independently verify historical claims in selected papers.

**S1-specific reproducibility concerns.** In the downloaded supplement, Web of Science combines title and topic conditions with AND; SciELO combines title and abstract conditions; PsycINFO also uses restrictive field conjunctions and labels a branch with MeSH. Separate rows/lines do not make the final cross-term Boolean combination unambiguous. The PubMed row contains a missing-space token, and S1's Scopus row lacks one plural-memory variant listed in the main text. OpenGrey syntax has punctuation/term irregularities. These are observations of the deposited description, not proof that those literal strings were executed or that eligible articles were missed.

If interpreted literally, conjunctive field restrictions can reduce sensitivity relative to searching either field. Historical interfaces, stemming, phrase handling, vocabulary mapping and actual query translation need checking before judging impact. A useful replication would preserve S1 unchanged, document each correction separately, retain native executed strings and hit counts, and compare retrieved identifiers. This appraisal did **not** rerun searches, estimate sensitivity, or reconstruct the original deduplication.

**Screening-count query.** The Results paragraph on p.373 leaves an arithmetic ambiguity: `1784 − 482 − 290 + 6 = 1018`; then `1018 − 940 = 78`, followed by mention of a later-added article. Treating that last addition as extra would give 79. It may already be included in an earlier total. Figure 1 was not visually verified and S2 is absent, so this is an **unresolved reporting question**, not a corrected count or demonstrated dataset error. Keep 78 explicitly attributed to the review. Resolution needs the rendered flowchart and a consistent selection ledger or author clarification.

**Eligibility and charting audit.** A database search hit is not automatically proof that its journal meets a particular indexing rule. Database coverage and indexing dates should be checked publication by publication; absence from Scopus alone would not invalidate eligibility through another named source. Reference harvesting from a specialist centre can improve recall while also inheriting its discovery network. The selection ledger should distinguish database retrieval, reference harvesting and later additions.

I did not locate a protocol/registration identifier, calibrated charting form, reproducible reviewer agreement record, or a design-specific critical-appraisal table in the inspected methods/S1. That is a reporting/access limit, not proof these procedures never existed. Do not equate general relevance screening or journal citation metrics with risk-of-bias assessment. [PRISMA-ScR items 12 and 16](https://www.prisma-statement.org/s/PRISMA-ScR-Fillable-Checklist_11Sept2019.pdf) make source appraisal conditional: its absence is not automatically noncompliance for a mapping review. Strong causal conclusions would nevertheless require it. Items 5, 7–10 and 14–17 provide useful reporting checks, rather than a numerical quality score.

## What the designs can and cannot establish

These are the appraiser's proposed inferential checks, not diagnoses of participants or findings that all included studies share a defect.

| Issue | Implication for inference | Case-specific check before stronger use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrospective case reports | Later reconstruction can preferentially preserve successful details and obscure chronology. A detailed narrative cannot supply its own comparison rate | Secure the earliest dated account; preserve original wording and revisions; record how candidates were discovered and all unsuccessful assertions |
| Interviews | Useful for documenting experience and witnesses, but repetition, leading questions, translation and family discussion can influence accounts | Neutral open prompts; separate interviews; original-language records and translation audit; document who heard what, when, and from whom |
| Ordinary information exposure | Prior-family separation does not close routes through neighbours, media, travel, relatives or intermediaries | Build a dated exposure/contact map including pre-interview and pre-match access; independently check plausible routes; leave untested exposure unknown |
| Source monitoring | A sincerely remembered detail can be attributed to the wrong source; felt certainty cannot identify its origin | Distinguish direct recollection, hearsay, inference, dreams and later learning; preserve provenance of each assertion; use appropriate comparison tasks without treating them as historical adjudication |
| Cultural concentration | Local beliefs may affect noticing, naming, referral and willingness to publish; research infrastructure also affects where cases are found | Compare recruitment and ascertainment across settings; involve local cultural expertise; avoid inferring either a cultural explanation or culture independence from geography alone |
| Selection and matching | Referral and publication can favour striking accounts; flexible candidate searches create many opportunities for partial correspondence | Track all approached/eligible accounts and refusals safely; prespecify match criteria and candidate universe; retain misses, ambiguities and decoys; avoid hindsight scoring |
| Limited independent verification | Several witnesses may repeat a shared story. Investigator location or a second paper does not establish independent evidence | Verify documents' dates/origins; use reviewers blinded to candidate status where feasible; map witness dependence and shared cases; distinguish replication of a pattern from independent confirmation of an information claim |

Source-monitoring is an alternative to test, not an explanation established for these cases. A [primary experimental abstract by Lindsay and Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2725271/) describes source-attribution effects in a laboratory eyewitness task. Only its abstract/metadata were read here. It supplies methodological background, not a measured error rate for spontaneous childhood reports. Adult regression experiments also cannot be transferred directly to this population.

The minority timing safeguard in Table 3 matters: it rules out a blanket claim that every report was recorded only after family contact. But “recorded before family contact” is weaker than “recorded before candidate identification,” and neither alone excludes every earlier ordinary source. A time-stamped statement can constrain later editing without establishing how its information was acquired. Conversely, an untested route is not evidence that a participant used it. Both overclaiming and premature dismissal should be avoided.

Case-control comparisons of psychological traits address those traits under their sampling conditions; they do not automatically test whether a historical match is accurate. Likewise, documentary analysis can establish that an event occurred while leaving unresolved whether the claimant had ordinary access to its description. Cohort follow-up can clarify changes over time without resolving information origin. No pooled effect, leakage-free case proportion, independent-case total or probability of reincarnation is estimated here.

| Question | What this appraisal supports | What would be additionally required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What literature was reported? | A bounded map with accessible methodological descriptors | S2 and native search/selection records for independent reconstruction |
| Does a specific account match a historical person? | No new adjudication | Earliest assertions, prespecified matching, records and independent case review |
| Was information obtained anomalously? | No new adjudication | Strong exclusion/testing of ordinary routes, selection/chance controls and independent replication |
| Did the same person or subject continue? | Not established | A separately specified identity criterion and evidence distinguishing it from information transfer or other alternatives |

Even a securely verified, unexpectedly accurate statement would not by itself determine personal identity, a transfer mechanism, or a metaphysical model. A shared phenotype or repeated narrative pattern is a different evidential target from uniquely identifying otherwise inaccessible information. The review's relevance to inquiry is not a promotion of any survival model.

## Implications for a proposed Sarnath inquiry

Use this appraisal to refine procedures, without assuming travel is confirmed or inviting personal cases into public Git history. Keep teachings, lived testimony and historical claims distinct. If an account arises voluntarily, obtain consent for its intended use before research recording; for children require appropriate guardian consent and assent, with safeguarding and local ethical oversight. Do not induce memories or pressure anyone to identify a previous life.

Before searching for a match, preserve a minimal, dated assertion record in approved private storage; document prior knowledge and contacts. Record misses and uncertainty as carefully as hits. Obtain separate permission for audio, translation, onward sharing and publication. Pseudonyms alone may not prevent identification from distinctive locations or events. Keep identities and contact details out of this repository; apply the project's existing restricted-access, retention and deletion rules. Respect community and cultural authority as well as individual consent. If those arrangements are absent, restrict activity to public educational observation and methodology preparation.
